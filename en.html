Every footprint counts

We are Salvemos sus Huellas («Let's Save Their Footprints»). We want young generations to meet the animals we are about to lose, understand why they are disappearing and discover what they can do to protect them.

The sixth extinction

This time, the cause is us

«The Earth has been through mass extinctions before, but this sixth crisis is different: it is being caused by humans.»

The question is no longer just which species are in danger, but whether we are willing to change our relationship with the planet to avoid losing them forever. The internet is where we learn the most today, and that is why we created this website: to inform, educate and inspire people to look after our planet.

Spheniscus demersus 📍 Coasts of South Africa and Namibia

One of the most threatened mammals in Europe. It lives along rivers and wetlands. Its great enemy is the American mink, escaped from fur farms, which takes its place and spreads disease.

Why they disappear

The five main causes

Scientists from the UN's global biodiversity platform (IPBES) identify five main causes, listed from the greatest to the smallest impact worldwide.

1. Habitat destruction

Cutting down forests, draining wetlands or turning nature into farmland, roads and cities leaves animals without a home or food. It is the number one cause.

2. Hunting, fishing and illegal trade

Poaching, overfishing and the trade in animals, skins, scales or exotic pets empty forests and seas. The pangolin and the beluga sturgeon are two examples.

3. Climate change

Warming melts the ice that polar bears depend on, pushes mountain species out of their homes and disrupts migrations, such as that of the monarch butterfly.

4. Pollution

Plastics, pesticides, spills and poisons get into the water and the food chain. The axolotl, the olm and vultures suffer from it directly.

5. Invasive species and disease

Animals and plants taken to places where they are not native push out local species, and diseases such as chytrid fungus spread around the world, wiping out amphibians.

Source: IPBES, Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity (2019).

There is hope

Conservation works

When a species and its habitat are protected, it can recover. These three animals from our list prove it.

Iberian lynx

From fewer than 100 animals at the start of the century to moving from Endangered to Vulnerable in 2024, thanks to breeding, protection and the recovery of rabbits.

Giant panda

Bamboo forest reserves in China allowed it to move from Endangered to Vulnerable in 2016.

Saiga antelope

After being on the brink of extinction, protection from hunting allowed it to recover, and in 2023 it moved to Near Threatened.

What you can do

Small steps, big footprints

You don't need to travel to the jungle to help. You can start doing these things today.

  1. Learn and share

    Talk about these species with your family and friends, and share this website on social media. What people don't know about, they don't protect.

  2. Don't buy exotic animals

    No exotic pets and no souvenirs made from skins, shells, ivory or scales. That trade fuels illegal trafficking.

  3. Shop wisely

    Choose sustainably caught fish, waste less food and check where the things you buy come from, such as palm oil.

  4. Less plastic, less pollution

    Reduce, reuse and recycle. Never leave litter in the countryside, rivers or the sea, and join beach or river clean-ups.

  5. Look after nature near you

    Respect natural areas, don't disturb animals and plant native flowers for pollinators such as butterflies.

  6. Do citizen science

    Photograph the wildlife you see and upload it to platforms such as iNaturalist: you help scientists know where species live.

  7. Save energy

    Walk, cycle or use public transport, and switch off what you are not using: slowing climate change also protects animals.

  8. Support conservation

    Volunteer or help organisations that protect species and habitats, like ours.

About us

Salvemos sus Huellas

We are an NGO dedicated to raising awareness among future generations about endangered animals, working to protect them and to care for their habitats.

Our main goal is to inform, educate and inspire people to look after our planet:

  • Inform with data from scientific sources.
  • Educate young people through the medium they use most: the internet.
  • Inspire with simple actions that anyone can take.

Cookies: this website does not set any cookies, neither its own nor third-party ones, so it does not need to ask for your consent.

Visitor statistics: to know how many people visit us and which sections interest them most, we use GoatCounter, a cookie-free visitor counter that does not store your IP address or any data that identifies you. It only records anonymous, aggregated data: pages viewed, which website you came from, device type and approximate country, and whether you use the search box, the filters or the challenge (without storing what you type or your answers).

Personal data: this website has no contact forms and does not collect names, email addresses or phone numbers.
